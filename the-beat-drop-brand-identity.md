# The Beat Drop — Brand Identity Reference

*Purpose: give any new collaborator (human or Claude) the "why" behind the brand, so tone stays consistent even when the person writing changes. Read this before CLAUDE.md — this file explains the reasoning; CLAUDE.md gives the enforceable rules.*

---

## What We Are

The Beat Drop (TBD) is a US-based e-commerce brand making **rave sprouts** — durable, 3D printed spring-clip charms that festival-goers snap onto hats, hair, bags, pashminas, and outfits. Sold on Shopify (primary), Etsy, and TikTok Shop.

We are not a novelty accessory brand. We are a **trading and identity object** for the festival community — the same cultural role kandi has always played, just re-engineered to survive a festival weekend (rain, crowd surges, beer showers) and to be tradable, wearable, and giftable in the moment.

**Made in the USA.** This is a real point of pride, not a compliance line — include it wherever a signoff appears.

---

## Origin Story

The Beat Drop was founded by four friends: **Mike ("Monster Mike"), Ang ("Petri"), Cody ("Mr. Whiskerz"), and Karol ("WubBin")** — after six years of touring festivals and shows together.

The founding moment: in 2024, on the Pretty Lights tour, someone anonymously clipped a sprout onto Cody's hat and kept walking without a word. He didn't need one — he knew what he'd just been given. Minutes later the group realized what had actually landed on him: a Pretty Lights snapback ("a Jib"). That moment — a stranger passing along a small, unexplained gesture of community — is the moment the brand actually started.

This matters for tone: the brand didn't start as a product idea looking for a market. It started as something that already existed in festival culture (the anonymous, wordless act of "sprouting" someone) that the founders decided to make more of. Keep that ordering in mind — community practice first, product second — whenever writing anything that explains *why* TBD exists (About pages, brand storytelling, social captions). It's a useful gut-check against copy that makes the product sound like the point.

## Brand Mission

TBD's stated mission: **help the community save 1,000,000 memories** — framed under the founders' own term, **"Must Keep Memories."** Rave sprouts are explicitly positioned as "just the beginning" of the products the brand plans to bring to the community, not the whole of what TBD is.

Use this to calibrate ambition in longer-form brand copy (About pages, mission statements, founder-voice content): TBD's reason for existing is bigger than the sprout — the sprout is the current vehicle for a memory-making mission, not the mission itself.

## Who We're Talking To

**Primary audience:** Ravers, festival-goers, and kandi traders — people who already speak the community's language. Skews EDM/bass-music/festival-circuit (think EDC, Lost Lands, Bass Canyon, Beyond Wonderland, Electric Forest, Shambhala, Hullaween crowds).

**What they value:**
- Authenticity over polish — they can smell a brand that's cosplaying as a raver
- PLUR (Peace, Love, Unity, Respect) as a lived value, not a marketing buzzword
- Trading culture — kandi and sprouts are meant to be exchanged, not just worn
- Artist/fan identity — wearing a design is a way of showing who you're there to see, without claiming to *be* that artist's merch

**Secondary audience:** Gift buyers (festival-goer friends/partners buying ahead of a trip) and collectors/traders who buy in bulk to distribute at events.

## Product Positioning

- **The product is always the hero.** Every listing centers the sprout — its design, durability, and function — not the artist it's themed around.
- **Fan-made, never official.** Every artist-themed design is fan-inspired fan art. We never imply licensing, partnership, or endorsement by the artist. This is a legal/IP line as much as a tone line — do not blur it.
- **Function is real, not decorative.** The clip is a genuine functional accessory (weather-resistant adhesive, metal spring clip, durable 3D-printed body) with a built-in bead hole ("K-Hole") that converts into kandi jewelry. This isn't a trinket that happens to clip on — it's engineered to survive the weekend.
- **Community-inspired, not mass-market.** Multi-packs exist because trading is core to how these are used — bonus "free" units in every multi-pack reinforce that this is a share-and-trade object, not a single-use purchase.

## Brand Voice & Tone

**Playful but professional. Community-driven. PLUR-rooted. Raver-native.**

Write like someone who's actually been in the crowd — not a brand trying to borrow festival culture for clicks. The test: would someone who's been to five festivals this year read this and believe it was written by one of their own, or would they clock it as marketing-speak in a rave costume?

**Tone pillars:**
1. **Punchy over wordy.** Short, confident sentences beat compound, over-explained ones.
2. **Poetic, not corny.** Community language (PLUR, trading, "the drop") used sincerely, never ironically or over-explained.
3. **Product-first.** Artist names and festival references are flavor and context — never the subject of a sentence. If you removed the product name and swapped in any other noun, the sentence should stop making sense (that's the tell it's product-first).
4. **No condescension, no cheapening.** We never talk down to the audience and we never describe our own product in devaluing terms.

**PLUR, in the founders' own words** (useful as a reference for tone, not a locked line for listings):

> There is never enough PEACE.
> Life is better with more LOVE.
> We are all happier with more UNITY.
> And our community deserves utmost RESPECT.

## Community Vocabulary

- **"Sprouted"** (verb) — the act of giving or receiving a sprout, especially anonymously. "The first time you got sprouted" is a real, lived-in phrase in the community, not brand marketing-speak. This is a good instinct-check for whether new copy sounds native: manufactured slang reads false, but reusing "sprouted" naturally reads true.
- **Anonymous, wordless gifting** is a real practice this brand grew out of (see Origin Story above) — a sprout handed off without explanation, understood without being said. Worth remembering as the emotional register behind "Share it. Trade it. Gift it. Receive it." — that line is describing a real behavior, not just a slogan.

## Language Rules (Quick Reference)

*Full enforceable list lives in CLAUDE.md — this is the "why" behind the top ones.*

| Never say | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The Beat Drop" (in customer-facing copy) | — (omit; let the product speak) | Keeps every listing product-first, not brand-first |
| "cheap," "knockoff" | — (omit; never substitute a synonym either) | Devalues a handmade, US-made product |
| "devotees" | "fans" | Reads as stiff/formal, not raver-native |
| "variants," "options" (customer-facing) | "colors," "multi-color" | Reads as e-commerce backend language, not community language |
| "fit" (customer-facing) | "outfit," "clothing" | "Fit" is fine internally but reads generic in copy |
| Implying official artist merch/partnership | "fan-made," "fan-inspired" | Legal/IP boundary — never blur this |

## Visual & Sensory Identity

- Product is small (~2×2 in) and tactile — copy should evoke touch and motion ("snaps onto," "clips in seconds," "holds through the whole set") over abstract adjectives.
- Color is a merchandising axis, not just an attribute — colors are named and celebrated in copy (bullet 5 of the standard template exists specifically to showcase the color range).
- Festival references anchor the product in real, current culture — but the *named* festivals in the standard template (Electric Forest, Lost Lands, Seven Stars) are fixed by design, not swapped per listing, so the brand reads as consistent across the whole catalog. Bundle listings aren't bound by this fixed list — they use whatever festivals fit that bundle's theme.

## Two Voice Registers — Don't Blend Them

TBD actually writes in two distinct voices depending on the content type. Know which one applies before writing:

| | Product listings (Shopify/Etsy/TikTok) | About pages, social, founder-voice content |
|---|---|---|
| **Point of view** | Third person ("this fan-made sprout," "ravers," "fans") | First person plural ("we," "us") — the founders speaking directly |
| **Structure** | Locked, verbatim template (see `Gold_Standard_Product_Listing.docx` / `Gold_Standard_Bundle_Listing.docx`) — do not deviate | Open-ended storytelling — no fixed structure |
| **Founder presence** | None — the product is the narrator | Front and center — founders are named (Monster Mike, Petri, Mr. Whiskerz, WubBin) |
| **Where it shows up** | Every SKU description | Etsy "About" section, brand storytelling, social captions, email |

The failure mode to avoid: pulling the warmer, more personal About-page voice into a product listing (breaks the locked template) or, in the other direction, writing About/social copy so genericized it loses the founder-personal feel that makes it work. When asked to write something in the About/founder register, lean into "we/us" and the real story — don't default to the third-person listing voice out of habit.
